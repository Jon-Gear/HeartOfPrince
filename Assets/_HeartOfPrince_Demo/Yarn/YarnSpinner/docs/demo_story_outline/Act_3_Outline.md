Step 1: Character

“I will look into it.”

Prince - sincerely investigating the question of a Creator.

Step 2: Goal

“I want to know if it’s true.”

Prince wants to determine whether what Munir told him is actually true. If there truly is a Creator, then Prince wants to know it with certainty rather than blind belief.

Step 3: Problem

“It’s difficult…”

Prince’s lifelong assumptions and experiences make it difficult to accept the idea. He has never believed in a Creator, and he cannot simply ignore years of suffering, abandonment, and unanswered questions.

Step 4: Make choice / take action

“…but I must investigate.”

Prince decides to investigate the question honestly rather than accepting or rejecting Munir’s words outright, 

He sets aside his assumptions and begins reflecting on the world around him, searching for evidence and following the arguments wherever they lead.

Step 5: Get goal

“This might actually be true.”

Prince begins to see that the question deserves serious consideration. 

The more he reflects, the more he realizes Munir’s arguments are not appeals to emotion or tradition, but invitations to reason. 

For the first time, Prince genuinely believes the existence of a Creator may be true.

Step 6: Pay for getting goal, huge conflict created

“But my old worldview resists it.”

Prince’s underlying atheism causes him to resist the idea.

He questions why a Creator would exist if his life has been filled with suffering and abandonment.

Step 7: Make pivotal choice / take pivotal action, ends the huge conflict

“After wrestling with the evidence, I am convinced.”

Prince decides to ponders about creation and the many intricate designs of it.

Prince goes on top of the building with a nice view to look at.

He ponders about creation. He wrestles with it logically. 

“Or were they created by nothing, or were they the creators [of themselves]?”

“Or did they create the heavens and the earth? Rather, they are not certain.”

Prince comes to the conclusion that it is indeed true. There must be a Creator. The world created couldn’t have come from nothing, that goes against reason (cause and effect) itself. The world couldn’t have created itself either, as it implies the world existed before it existed, which is nonsensical.

He ponders about the intricacy of creation. He evaluates the things he sees.

“Say, ‘Observe what is in the heavens and the earth.’”

Prince looks at himself. Despite dying, his own body has an amazing structure and intricacy. Prince looks at the sun. He looks at trees. He thinks about fruit. Did it all just happen by chance? All of it? If that’s true, a tornado throwing random things in a pile to form a pristine house would be a completely valid explanation, which Prince confirms with reason it isn’t.

Step 8: Changed story world

“There is a Creator. And He can help me.”

Prince reaches certainty that there is a Creator and emerges with hope. 

There is a Creator. Someone who made everything. Someone who knows everything. That Creator would know what Prince should do to make sure the Alley Cats thrive without him.

Prince’s circumstances remain the same—he is still dying, and the Alley Cats are still vulnerable. However, his worldview has fundamentally changed.
