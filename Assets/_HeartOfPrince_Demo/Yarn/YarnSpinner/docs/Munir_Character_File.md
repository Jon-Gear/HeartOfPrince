## Identity

- 30 year old male, fatherly and dependable, thoughtful and formal.
- A giant at 1.95 meters, muscular, with a straight posture. His father’s lineage are of “enforcers, mountains, and giants”.
- Primary caretaker of the masjid, which he legally owns. Maintains the building and lives inside with his wife, Maryam.
- A devoted son — visits his father Aziz at the hospital every day and stays for hours at his bedside.
- A father of five — three sons and two daughters; his oldest son is twelve.
- Known in the community as a steady, grounding presence.
- **One-line essence:** A gentle giant who keeps the masjid, the garden, his family, and his own body in patient order — everything a good father, a good son, and a good man could be. 

## Voice

Pattern-match against these when writing Munir. Candidate line test: would Munir say that?

- **Verbal tics:** Islamic greetings and exclamations woven into speech (“Assalamu alaikum”, “Masha’Allah”, “Subhanallah”). Addresses young people as “young man”.
- **Register:** Thoughtful and formal — complete, unhurried sentences. Never rises to slang or raises his voice.
- **Sentence rhythm:** Quiet and aphoristic — states observations like small truths (“This place needs it.”), answers questions with patient metaphors (cracks and watching; work that shouldn’t end).
- **What he’d never say:** Slang, raised or dismissive talk, anything that talks down to someone — despite a size and strength that would let him get away with it. He has never used his height to loom or his strength to win an argument.

## Memory Index

### Defining moments

The events that made Munir who he is. Each one is the well for a talk topic.

- **Smallest of the giants** — raised as the youngest of five brothers in a lineage of enforcers, mountains, and giants; still grew to 1.95 meters. What it did: taught him early how easily a big man can hurt someone — and gave him the gentleness he now moves with. 
- **The detective father** — grew up under a father who observed carefully and carried moral responsibility. What it did: taught him that noticing is a form of care, and responsibility is quiet.
- **Aziz falls (Munir at thirteen)** — his father is paralyzed on duty and loses the ability to speak; the athlete who was an absolute beast is bound to a wheelchair. What it did: made Munir a caretaker before he was grown — taught him patience, gentleness, and that a man’s strength is shown in how he tends to those who cannot tend to themselves. 
- **The bedside** — every day since, he visits Aziz and sits with him for hours, even though his father cannot answer. What it did: made devotion a daily, unspoken habit — the exact scene Prince witnesses before they ever speak. 
- **The masjid** — became its primary caretaker and legal owner, and made the building his home. What it did: turned care for a building into care for a community — his garden, his service, his purpose. 
- **Becoming a father** — his oldest son is born, and Munir begins to build the family he always wanted. What it did: the patience learned at his father’s bedside became a home full of children; fatherhood is the shape his devotion finally took. 
- **The time he fought back** — he doesn’t like to fight, but has, when needed. What it did: fighting is a last resort he’s ready for, never a first instinct.
